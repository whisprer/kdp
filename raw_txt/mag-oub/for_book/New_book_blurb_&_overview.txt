Jacket-Style Summary (short, ~200 words)

Humanity has ended before. It will end again.
Every twenty-four thousand years, the cycle begins anew: civilizations rise, collapse, burn to ash — and forget.

In the wake of the Moon’s wounding comet, billions are blinded, surface societies fracture into ash-chanting prophets and technocratic Overseers sealed in underground vaults. Beneath Anatolia, in the ancient citadel of Derinkuyu, a girl named Lena inhales the bitter smoke of a resin distilled from magnolia and camphor hybrids. The visions nearly kill her.

The resin is no simple drug. It is both key and chain, the mortar of an invisible prison — the Magnolia Oubliette — in which humanity has been trapped for ages, blind to its own captivity. Each inhalation binds minds into recursive loops, each generation willingly marching back into the serpent’s coils.

But Lena is different. Named by the citadel’s Obelisk, she becomes a Navigator, able to steer not only ships through visions but human psyches themselves. Across the stars, the smuggler freighter Papa Samuel carries a coffin-cargo humming with impossible equations, its crew spiraling into the same dream.

When sacrifice at Lagrange saves billions, Lena glimpses what no Navigator has before: a door beyond the coils.

The oubliette is not unbreakable.

Story Spine (expanded, ~500 words)

Act I: Collapse. Civilization crumbles not in firestorms but in slope — reservoirs drained, cities emptied, memes whispering “check the Moon.” Then comes the comet: the Moon is scarred, billions are blinded, surface societies fragment. Blind caravans become the Ash Prophets, preaching blindness as freedom. The Overseers entomb themselves in fortified vaults, ruling by decree and drone.

Act II: Citadel. In Derinkuyu, Lena is pulled into the resin’s visions — coils, serpents, circuits — and learns the drug’s origin: a magnolia-camphor hybrid, corrupted by fungus and chemical poisons. Beautiful, perfumed, and deadly, it is both religion and shackle. The arrival of two neural-linked survivors, the Custodians, awakens the Obelisk, which speaks Lena’s name. Yoshihiro reveals the truth: resin is the mortar of humanity’s oubliette, locking minds into recursive 24k-year cycles of rise and fall.

Act III: Surface & Stars. The Ash Prophets swell in number, Overseers crush them mercilessly at Kufra, feeding resin ash into their chants. Overseers themselves fracture into Pragmatists and Visionaries as serpent dreams seep into their vats. Meanwhile, the freighter Papa Samuel, known as the Lantern Ghost, carries a sealed coffin-cargo humming equations. Its crew, led by Sal, begin dreaming serpent visions, their ship glowing like a lantern in the dark.

Act IV: Revelation & Sacrifice. Lena embraces the resin, no longer fleeing its coils but steering minds out of loops — her first act as Navigator. On the Papa Samuel, Sal recognizes the coffin not as freight but as seed. Pursued by Overseer swarms at the Lagrange Point, she and the Samuel sacrifice themselves to save billions. The Falcon — a reborn ship seeded from the coffin — escapes into the dark. Lena collapses in grief, her weakness briefly reinforcing the oubliette’s bars. But in her final trance she sees not coils, not cages — but a door.

End of Book 1: Humanity remains bound, but the door gleams. For the first time in twenty-four thousand years, the oubliette might be broken.
