Proofreader’s Guide — The Magnolia Oubliette (Draft II)
1. Formatting & Consistency

Chapter headings: Some are formatted as
The Magnolia Oubliette / Chapter X: Title
Others as just Chapter X: Title.
→ Please flag any inconsistencies and suggest a standard form.

Installments in Chapter One: Currently labeled as (installment one, two, three, four).
→ Should these be merged into one seamless chapter, or left as titled fragments?

Section breaks: Confirm all horizontal rules and “=======” separators are consistently applied.

2. Continuity & Lore

Custodians: Check for consistency in how the Neuralink pair are described (arrival, disappearance, transformation). Ensure no contradictions between earlier mentions and their later roles as “voices in the citadel.”

Papulegara / serpent mythology: Verify references remain consistent (is it serpent/dragon/coil/etc.) and not drifting into confusing terminology.

Comet/Moon arc: Ensure that “Not the comet. Not yet.” → Dead Internet → Moon blindness → citadel survival flows smoothly without any missing link.

3. Tone & Style

Voice: The prose is intentionally mythic, lyrical, recursive. Please flag any passages where the tone slips into too modern, casual, or expository compared to the surrounding text.

Refrains: Repeated phrases like “Not the comet. Not yet.”, “The serpent coils.”, and “The coil turns again.” are deliberate motifs. Confirm they are consistent, not mistyped, and always effective.

4. Placement of Non-Narrative Material

Papa Samuel Ship Technicals: These sections are written in encyclopedia/technical style and may feel abrupt mid-narrative.
→ Please comment on whether they work in place, or if they’d be better as an appendix/end-matter.

Hymns, fragments, myths: Check formatting (italics, stanza spacing) for readability and consistency.

5. Mechanics

Grammar & Syntax: Correct any spelling errors, misplaced punctuation, or grammar that breaks flow — but preserve intentional archaic or poetic phrasing.

Paragraph length: Highlight any sections where excessively long blocks might benefit from spacing for readability.

Typos in names/terms: Double-check spelling of recurring names (e.g., Papulegara, Yoshihiro, Derinkuyu).

6. General Reader Impressions

Does the narrative make sense straight through without requiring prior knowledge?

Are there any moments of confusion where a reader might lose track of plot, character, or lore?

Are transitions between major arcs (collapse → blindness → citadel → surface cults) smooth enough, or do any feel abrupt?
